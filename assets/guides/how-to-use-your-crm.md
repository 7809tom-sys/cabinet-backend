# How to Use Your CRM Effectively
## Turn Your Pipeline Into Predictable Sales

> Your CRM (Customer Relationship Management) system is the single most important tool for growing your cabinet business. Affiliates who use it consistently close more deals, waste less time, and never lose a lead to forgetfulness. This guide shows you exactly how to use it.

---

## What Is a CRM and Why Does It Matter?

A CRM is a system for tracking every person you are selling to — where they are in the buying process, what you last said to them, and what your next step is. Without a CRM, leads fall through the cracks. With a CRM, every lead has a next action and a follow-up date.

The Cabinet Dealz CRM is built directly into your affiliate dashboard. You do not need any external software. Every lead you add is visible only to you.

---

## The 5 Pipeline Stages

Your CRM uses a visual pipeline — a board with five columns, one for each stage of the sales process. Every lead (called a "deal") lives in exactly one stage at a time.

| Stage | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| **New Lead** | You have their contact info but have not yet made contact | Reach out within 24 hours |
| **Contacted** | You have spoken with them or sent your storefront link | Follow up in 3 days if no response |
| **Quoted** | You have sent them a price or walked through the design tool | Follow up in 5–7 days |
| **Negotiating** | They are interested but have questions, concerns, or are comparing options | Stay in contact every 3–5 days |
| **Closed / Won** | They placed an order | Request a referral 2–3 weeks after delivery |

There is also a **Lost** status for leads who are no longer active. Do not delete lost leads — mark them lost and move on. They may come back in 6–12 months.

## How to Add a Lead

Add a new lead the moment you meet someone who might be interested — do not wait until you get home. The best time to add a lead is while the conversation is still fresh.

**What to enter:**
- **Name** — first and last
- **Phone** — the number you will text or call
- **Email** — for sending quotes and follow-ups
- **Stage** — start at "New Lead" unless you have already made contact
- **Notes** — anything relevant: "3-bedroom house, wants to update kitchen and two bathrooms, budget around $12,000, timeline 60 days"

**The golden rule of CRM notes:** Write down everything you would want to remember if you had not spoken to this person in 30 days. Because in 30 days, you might not remember.

## The Follow-Up Cadence

The biggest mistake salespeople make is giving up too early. Research consistently shows that most sales require 5 to 12 contacts before a decision is made. Most salespeople give up after 2.

Your CRM makes it easy to stay in contact systematically without being annoying.

**Recommended cadence by stage:**

**New Lead → Contacted:**
- Contact within 24 hours of adding them
- If no response, follow up at day 3 and day 7
- If still no response, move to a monthly check-in

**Contacted → Quoted:**
- Send the storefront link and design tool invitation within 24 hours
- Follow up 3 days later: "Did you get a chance to look at the site?"
- Follow up 7 days later with a room scene photo or style suggestion

**Quoted → Negotiating:**
- Follow up 5 days after sending the quote
- Ask specifically: "Do you have any questions about the quote?"
- Address any objections (see the 10 Steps to a Sale guide)

**Negotiating → Closed:**
- Follow up every 3–5 days
- Each contact should add value: a new photo, a customer story, an answer to a question
- Ask for the order directly at least once per week

## Writing Good Notes

Your notes are the memory of your sales process. A good note tells you exactly what happened and what to do next.

**Bad note:** "Called, no answer"

**Good note:** "Called at 2pm, no answer. Left voicemail. Will text tomorrow. She mentioned last week she was waiting on her contractor to confirm the start date — check back on that."

**After every contact, write:**
1. What you did (called, texted, met in person)
2. What they said
3. What the next step is and when

## Your Weekly CRM Review

Set aside 15 minutes every Monday morning to review your pipeline. This is the most valuable 15 minutes of your sales week.

**Ask yourself for each deal:**
- What stage is this in?
- When did I last contact them?
- What is my next step?
- Is there anything blocking this deal from moving forward?

Move any deal that has been in the same stage for more than 2 weeks to the top of your priority list. Stalled deals need attention.

---

## Your Monthly Pipeline Report

Once a month, look at your pipeline from a higher level:

- How many deals are in each stage?
- How many deals did I close this month?
- How many new leads did I add?
- What is my conversion rate from Contacted to Closed?

If you are adding lots of leads but few are closing, the problem is in your follow-up or your close. Go back to Steps 7 and 8 of the 10 Steps to a Sale guide.

If you are not adding enough new leads, the problem is in your prospecting. Go back to Step 1.

---

## Using the CRM to Track Referrals

Every closed deal is an opportunity for a referral. Add a reminder in the CRM to follow up with every customer 2–3 weeks after their order is delivered.

**Note to add at close:** "Order placed [date]. Follow up [date + 3 weeks] to check on delivery and ask for referrals."

When you follow up, ask directly: "Do you know anyone else who is thinking about updating their kitchen? I would love an introduction."

One referral from a happy customer is worth more than 10 cold outreach attempts.

## CRM Best Practices — Quick Reference

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Add every lead immediately | Wait until later to add leads |
| Write detailed notes after every contact | Leave notes blank or vague |
| Set a next action for every deal | Leave deals without a next step |
| Review your pipeline every Monday | Only look at it when you remember |
| Follow up 5–12 times before giving up | Give up after 2 attempts |
| Mark lost deals as lost (don't delete) | Delete leads who don't buy immediately |
| Ask for referrals from every closed deal | Forget to follow up after the sale |
