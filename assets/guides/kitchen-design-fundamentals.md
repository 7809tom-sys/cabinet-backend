## Part 1: How to Measure Your Kitchen

Accurate measurements are the foundation of every successful cabinet order. Mistakes in measurement lead to gaps, overhangs, or cabinets that simply do not fit.

### What You Need
- A metal tape measure (cloth tapes stretch and give inaccurate readings)
- Graph paper or the Cabinet Dealz 2D design tool
- A pencil and notepad

### What to Measure

**Floor plan dimensions:**
- Measure each wall from corner to corner at counter height (36")
- Note the location and width of every window and door opening
- Note which direction doors swing (this affects cabinet placement)
- Measure the distance from the floor to the bottom of any windows (this determines upper cabinet height)
- Note the ceiling height

**Existing features to document:**
- Location of the sink (and which wall the plumbing is on)
- Location of the range or cooktop
- Location of the refrigerator
- Location of electrical outlets, switches, and light fixtures
- Location of any soffits or bulkheads above the cabinets

**Pro tip:** Measure twice. Always double-check your numbers before placing an order.

## Part 2: Standard Cabinet Dimensions

Understanding standard sizes helps you plan a layout that works without custom modifications.

### Base Cabinets

| Dimension | Standard Size |
|---|---|
| Height | 34.5" (plus countertop = 36" finished) |
| Depth | 24" |
| Width range | 9" to 48" (in 3" increments) |

### Wall (Upper) Cabinets

| Dimension | Standard Size |
|---|---|
| Height | 30", 36", or 42" |
| Depth | 12" |
| Width range | 9" to 48" (in 3" increments) |
| Mounting height | 18" above countertop (standard) |

### Tall / Pantry Cabinets

| Dimension | Standard Size |
|---|---|
| Height | 84", 90", or 96" |
| Depth | 24" (pantry) or 12" (oven surround) |
| Width range | 18" to 36" |

### Countertop Overhang
Standard countertop overhangs the base cabinet by **1.5"** on the front and sides (except against walls).

## Part 3: The Work Triangle

The kitchen work triangle is the relationship between the three primary work zones: the **refrigerator**, the **sink**, and the **range**. An efficient kitchen keeps these three points close enough to minimize walking but far enough apart to allow two people to work comfortably.

**Guidelines:**
- Each leg of the triangle should be between **4 and 9 feet**
- The total perimeter of the triangle should be between **13 and 26 feet**
- No major traffic path should cross through the triangle

In open-plan kitchens and galley layouts, the triangle concept adapts — but the principle of keeping the three work zones accessible to each other remains the same.

## Part 4: Common Kitchen Layouts

| Layout | Best For | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Galley** | Narrow spaces, efficiency | Two parallel runs of cabinets; excellent workflow |
| **L-Shape** | Medium kitchens | Two adjacent walls; good corner use with lazy Susan or blind corner |
| **U-Shape** | Large kitchens | Three walls; maximum storage; requires 8'+ width |
| **G-Shape** | Large kitchens | U-shape with peninsula; great for two cooks |
| **Single Wall** | Small spaces, studios | All cabinets on one wall; limited storage |
| **Island** | Open-plan kitchens | Adds prep space and seating; needs 42"–48" clearance around it |

## Part 5: The 10×10 Kitchen — Your Baseline Reference

The industry standard for comparing cabinet pricing is the **10×10 kitchen**: two walls of cabinets, each 10 feet long, forming an L-shape.

**A standard 10×10 kitchen includes:**
- 12 base cabinets (various widths totaling 10 linear feet per wall)
- 12 wall cabinets (matching the base layout)
- No island, no specialty pieces, no trim or crown molding

This configuration is used as a baseline because it allows apples-to-apples price comparison between cabinet lines. When a customer asks "how much does a kitchen cost?" — start with the 10×10 as a reference point, then adjust for their actual layout.

**What is NOT included in a 10×10 price:**
- Crown molding and light rail
- Lazy Susan or blind corner accessories
- Pull-out trays and organizers
- Tall / pantry cabinets
- Island cabinets
- Shipping

## Part 6: Upper Cabinet Height — 30" vs. 36" vs. 42"

The height of the upper cabinets dramatically affects the look and storage of the kitchen.

| Height | Ceiling Clearance | Look | Storage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30" | 8' ceiling with soffit, or 9'+ with gap | Traditional | Standard |
| 36" | 9' ceiling standard | Modern, spacious | More |
| 42" | 9'–10' ceiling | High-end, dramatic | Maximum |

**For 8-foot ceilings:** 30" uppers with a soffit above, or 36" uppers that go to the ceiling (no soffit needed).

**For 9-foot ceilings:** 42" uppers are the most popular choice — they reach near the ceiling and eliminate the dust-collecting gap.

## Part 7: Corner Solutions

Corners are the most challenging part of any kitchen layout. There are four main options:

**Blind corner base cabinet:** One cabinet extends into the corner; the other butts up against it. The "blind" section is accessible but requires reaching. Most economical option.

**Lazy Susan:** A rotating shelf system inside a 33"–36" corner cabinet. Easy access, popular with homeowners.

**Magic corner / Le Mans:** A pull-out system that brings items from the back of the corner to the front. Premium option, excellent accessibility.

**Dead corner with drawer stack:** Fill the corner with a tall drawer stack and skip the corner cabinet entirely. Works well in modern kitchens.

## Part 9: What to Order — A Quick Checklist

Before submitting any order, confirm the following with your customer:

- [ ] All measurements verified (measured twice)
- [ ] Door style selected
- [ ] Finish/color confirmed
- [ ] Corner solution decided (blind corner, lazy Susan, or other)
- [ ] Sink base size confirmed (matches sink dimensions)
- [ ] Range/cooktop base confirmed (matches appliance width)
- [ ] Refrigerator panel or end panel needed?
- [ ] Crown molding and light rail included?
- [ ] Shipping address confirmed
- [ ] Installation timeline confirmed

A complete, accurate order avoids delays, returns, and unhappy customers.
