---

## Bottom line

Our current **homeowner** prices are running roughly **1.6x to 2.6x** what the major online cabinet sellers actually charge a homeowner at checkout. On a representative 10‑item basket, we sit at **$7,197** versus a market street median of **$4,058** — we are about **77% above market**. To be competitive (the goal you set), homeowner prices should come down to roughly the market median, which is about a **44% reduction** on these items.

Importantly, **our cost floor leaves plenty of room**: even after cutting to ~5% under the market median, every item still holds a **41%–61% gross margin** over our hard cost. We do not have to sell at a loss to be competitive.

---

## How I shopped

I pulled real, current checkout (sale) prices for **white shaker, RTA, plywood box, soft‑close** cabinets — the closest like‑for‑like to our mainstream lines — from eight sellers: **Lily Ann Cabinets, The RTA Store, RTA Cabinet Store, Kitchen Cabinet Kings, Kabinet King, Discount Kitchen Direct, Cabinet Distribution, BuyWholesaleCabinets,** plus **Home Depot (Hampton Bay, assembled)** as a retail ceiling. I also found a **Faircrest Cabinets** B24 listed at $496.94 MSRP on thewhiteshaker.com — useful since Faircrest is one of our own lines.

These sites run perpetual 35–53% "sales," so the **sale price is the real market price** a homeowner pays — that is what I compared against, not the inflated struck‑through "regular" price.

## What I recommend

1. **Reprice the homeowner tier to ~5% under the market street median**, applied as a category‑level rule rather than ten hand‑typed numbers, with a hard floor of **cost × 1.7** so margin is always protected. This lands us slightly under the most aggressive national sellers while still holding 40%+ margin.
2. **Keep contractor and wholesale tiers as‑is** (they already sit below homeowner and are not the competitive problem).
3. **Present it the way competitors do**: show the higher "list/MSRP" struck through with the homeowner price as the sale price, so the value is obvious without eroding perceived quality.
4. **Re‑check quarterly** — these competitors change "sale" depth often; I can re‑run this shop on a schedule.
