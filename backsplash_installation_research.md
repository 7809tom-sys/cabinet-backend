# Backsplash Pattern and Grout Construction Research

## Owner’s Required Designer Rule

Render backsplash products as their **actual connected pattern field**, rather than as repeated framed 12-inch product-card squares. Use a **1/16-inch grout joint** for the supported rectified/mosaic backsplash presentation requested by the owner. The product’s individual tile, interlocking mosaic, or sheet module remains continuous through the whole supported run, with only true wall/end/upstand cuts interrupting it.

## Construction Findings

The Tile Council of North America explains that ANSI A108.02 expects nominal joint centerlines to be straight and evenly sized, while actual joint width must account for tile dimensions, edge variation, material, and substrate. It also notes that cementitious joints below 1/8 inch generally use unsanded grout, and identifies 1/16-inch spacing as a normal tight-spacing example for tightly manufactured marble. These findings support a stable, even **1/16-inch** visual joint only for product patterns that have clean, rectified or mosaic geometry. [1]

Tile Shop’s mosaic installation guidance describes installing sheets on a flat substrate and bedding them evenly with a grout float or flat block. The renderer should therefore treat a mosaic sheet as a source field that continues across sheet boundaries—not as separate, visibly framed catalog-card squares. [2]

Fireclay’s manufacturer guidance states that grout width is product- and edge-dependent: its published range is 1/16 to 3/16 inch, and its 1/16 recommendation is for clean-edged glass tile; handmade material needs wider joints to accommodate variation. The Designer must preserve the owner-selected 1/16-inch rule as the default for eligible clean-edged/interlocking products, while retaining product-specific pattern geometry rather than imposing a universal square-grid overlay. [3]

## Renderer Requirements Derived From Research

| Product pattern type | Continuous field rule | Visual joint rule | Cut behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rectified field tile | Continue tile courses over all connected, countertop-supported backsplash segments. | 1/16-inch straight, even grout grid. | Clip only at run ends, upper-cabinet/hood limits, openings, and optional rear upstand. |
| Subway/brick pattern | Continue the row offset across segment boundaries; do not restart at each cabinet. | 1/16-inch horizontal and vertical joints. | Preserve partial end tiles and top cut courses. |
| Interlocking mosaic | Use the material’s repeated interlocking module with no sheet-outline seam. | Retain the source’s individual mosaic joints at 1/16-inch visual spacing where applicable. | Crop the continuous module only at true physical boundaries. |
| Sheet-mounted mosaic | Repeat only the internal material field, not white borders, labels, or the complete 12-inch card. | No artificial square sheet frame; show only individual tile/mosaic joints. | Cut the continuous field at physical boundaries. |

## Owner-Selected Desert Clay Pattern Evidence

Pelican identifies the saved selection, **Desert Clay**, as an 11.81 × 11.81 × 8 mm glass-and-stainless-steel mosaic for wall use without water. The product page provides three full-resolution images (`DesertClay_907x680.png`, `5.DesertClay-D_1020x680.jpg`, and `5.DesertClay_684x680.jpg`) that are suitable for determining the true repeated mosaic module. The current Designer must derive its continuous interlocking field from those pattern images, not from the square catalog card or from an imposed square grid. [4]

Visual review of Pelican’s full-resolution Desert Clay field image shows a sheet-mounted composition of small aligned glass-and-stainless mosaic tiles in varying amber, brown, cream, charcoal, and stone finishes. It is not a 12-inch field tile: the 11.81-inch sheet contains many small modules. The renderer must therefore repeat the **interior mosaic field** continuously at the product sheet scale, retain only the fine internal joints, and omit any larger square outline that would reveal the individual sheet or catalog-card boundary.
