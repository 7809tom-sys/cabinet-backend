# 10 Steps to a Sale
## Your Cabinet Dealz Affiliate Sales Playbook

> Affiliates who follow this 10-step system consistently report a **35% increase in sales** compared to those who rely on word-of-mouth alone. These steps are not theory — they are the proven actions that turn conversations into commissions.

---

## Step 1: Prospect — Find Your Next Customer

Every sale starts with a conversation. Your best prospects are people who are already thinking about their kitchen.

**Where to find them:**
- Homeowners posting in local Facebook groups about remodeling
- Contractors and builders who need a reliable cabinet source for their clients
- Real estate agents staging homes for sale (kitchens sell houses)
- Interior designers looking for a trade partner
- Neighbors, friends, and family — your warm market is your fastest market
- Home improvement stores — people browsing cabinet displays are actively shopping
- New construction neighborhoods — knock on doors during the build phase

**Your goal at this step:** Get a name, a phone number, and a rough idea of their timeline.

---

## Step 2: Make Contact — Introduce Yourself and Your Store

Your first contact sets the tone. Keep it simple, friendly, and focused on them — not on cabinets.

**Text script:**
> "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Your Store Name]. I help homeowners get beautiful kitchen cabinets at contractor pricing — without the contractor markup. Do you have 5 minutes this week to take a look at what I offer?"

**Phone script:**
> "Hi [Name], I'm [Your Name] — I run a cabinet store called [Your Store Name]. I work with homeowners who are remodeling their kitchens and want quality cabinets at a fair price. Are you or anyone you know thinking about updating their kitchen?"

**Key rule:** Always end with a question. Questions keep the conversation going.

---

## Step 3: Qualify — Ask the Right Questions

Before you invest time in a prospect, make sure they are a real buyer. A qualified lead has a timeline, a budget, and a decision to make.

**The four qualifying questions:**
1. "What's your timeline — are you hoping to start in the next 30, 60, or 90 days?"
2. "Do you have a rough budget in mind for the cabinets?"
3. "Is this a full kitchen replacement or are you updating specific areas?"
4. "Are you working with a contractor, or doing the installation yourself?"

A prospect who says "sometime next year, not sure on budget" is a long-term nurture. A prospect who says "we want to start in 6 weeks and have $8,000 for cabinets" is a hot lead — move fast.

---

## Step 4: Share Your Link — Send Them to Your Storefront

Once you have a qualified prospect, send them your storefront link immediately. This is your 24/7 showroom.

**Text to send:**
> "Here's my store — you can browse all the door styles and see pricing right on the site: [your storefront URL]. The design tool lets you plan your whole kitchen layout. Let me know what styles catch your eye and I'll put together a quote."

**Why this works:** Your storefront is branded to you, shows your prices, and has the design tool built in. It does the selling while you sleep.

---

## Step 5: Walk the Design Tool — Show Them How to Plan Their Kitchen

The 2D kitchen design tool is your most powerful sales asset. Customers who use it are far more likely to buy because they can see their kitchen before they commit.

**How to use it as a sales tool:**
- Offer to do a screen share or in-person demo
- Start with their actual kitchen dimensions (have them measure first — see the Kitchen Design Fundamentals guide)
- Place the cabinets together and show them how the layout works
- Let them pick door styles and see the room scene photos
- Save the design and use it as the basis for their quote

**Script:** "Let me show you how the design tool works — it takes about 10 minutes and at the end you'll have a real layout and a price. Want to do that now or schedule a time?"

---

## Step 6: Present the Quote — Walk Through Pricing Clearly

Once the design is done, the quote is built from it. Walk the customer through it line by line.

**How to present:**
- Start with the total: "Your complete kitchen in [door style] comes to $X."
- Break it down by section: uppers, lowers, tall cabinets, specialty pieces
- Remind them what's included: all boxes, doors, hardware, soft-close hinges
- Address shipping: "Shipping is calculated based on your ZIP code and will be added at checkout."

**Never apologize for the price.** Your pricing is competitive with big box stores but the quality is significantly better. If they push back, go to Step 7.

---

## Step 7: Handle Objections — Turn "No" Into "Not Yet"

Every sale has objections. The customer who objects is still engaged — they just need more information or reassurance.

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "It's too expensive." | "Compared to what? Let me show you what the same kitchen costs at [big box store]. Our price is usually 20–40% less for better quality." |
| "I need to think about it." | "Of course — what's the main thing you want to think through? I want to make sure you have everything you need to decide." |
| "I'm not sure about the quality." | "That's a fair question. Our cabinets are [all-plywood construction / dovetail joints / soft-close standard]. Would it help to see some photos of installed kitchens?" |
| "I want to get other quotes." | "Absolutely — you should. When you get them, compare the box construction (plywood vs. particleboard) and what's included. I'm confident we'll be competitive." |
| "I need to talk to my spouse." | "Of course. Would it help if I sent you a summary you could share with them? Or we could do a quick call together." |

---

## Step 8: Close — Ask for the Order

Most salespeople lose the sale because they never ask for it. Closing is simply asking the customer to make a decision.

**Simple closes:**
- "Are you ready to move forward? I can get your order placed today."
- "If the pricing works for you, the next step is just a deposit to lock in your order. Want to do that now?"
- "What would it take for you to feel comfortable moving forward today?"

**The deposit close:** "We require a 50% deposit to begin processing your order. The balance is due before shipping. Want me to send you the payment link?"

If they say yes — stop talking and process the order. If they say no — ask what's stopping them and go back to Step 7.

---

## Step 9: Follow Up — Never Let a Lead Go Cold

Most sales happen on the 5th to 12th contact. Most salespeople give up after 2. Your CRM is your follow-up system — use it.

**The follow-up cadence:**
- **Day 1:** Send storefront link + design tool invitation
- **Day 3:** Check in — "Did you get a chance to look at the site? Any questions?"
- **Day 7:** Share a room scene photo or a customer story — "Just wanted to show you what [door style] looks like installed…"
- **Day 14:** "Just following up — are you still planning to update your kitchen?"
- **Day 30:** "Checking in — any updates on your timeline? We have some new styles you might like."
- **Day 60:** "Still here when you're ready. Kitchen projects often take longer to start than expected — no pressure."

**Key rule:** Always add a note to your CRM after every contact. If you don't write it down, it didn't happen.

---

## Step 10: Get Referrals — Your Best Leads Come From Happy Customers

After every successful installation, ask for referrals. A happy customer is your best marketing.

**Script (send by text 2–3 weeks after delivery):**
> "Hey [Name], I hope the kitchen is coming together beautifully! If you know anyone else who's thinking about updating their kitchen, I'd love an introduction. I'll take great care of them — and there's a thank-you gift in it for you."

**Make it easy:** Give them your digital business card link to forward. One text from a happy customer is worth more than 10 cold calls.
